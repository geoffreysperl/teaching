## Thesis Research

My current research focuses on the effects of tabletop roleplaying games (TTRPGs) in the classroom on the academic literacy of secondary students. This work examines how structured roleplay can enhance student engagement and literacy development through:

* Reading comprehension and critical analysis
* Vocabulary development and language skills
* Writing skills and creative expression
* Narrative competence and storytelling

This research bridges theoretical foundations from play theory, developmental psychology, and literacy research with practical classroom applications, offering a framework for educators interested in game-based learning approaches.

## Character Analysis Role-Playing (CARP)

During my student teaching in 2023-24, I developed and implemented a lesson using concepts from tabletop role-playing games to analyze characters in "The Most Dangerous Game." By adapting a solo TTRPG called "The Last Tea Shop Complete," I created an engaging approach to character analysis where students interviewed potential heroes or villains.

This innovative method achieved 100% participation and resulted in creative audio recordings from students, demonstrating how game-based approaches can transform traditionally passive learning activities into dynamic, engaging experiences.

You can download the game instructions for free on [Teachers Pay Teachers](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Character-Analysis-Roleplaying-CARP-The-Most-Dangerous-Game-11492335).

## Educational Technology Perspective

My 25 years in technology before transitioning to education give me a unique perspective on educational technology use. I am both technologically proficient and critically aware of technology's limitations in the learning process. This background allows me to make thoughtful decisions about when and how to incorporate digital tools.

My approach to technology integration can be summarized as:

> Technology should enhance human connection, not replace it. Digital tools should be used intentionally and strategically, serving specific learning goals rather than being used for their own sake.

Through my research and classroom innovations, I strive to develop approaches that honor traditional literacy skills while preparing students for an increasingly digital future.
