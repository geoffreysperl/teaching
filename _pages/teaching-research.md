## Thesis Research

I'm currently exploring how tabletop roleplaying games (TTRPGs) in the classroom affect the academic literacy of secondary students. This work examines how structured roleplay can transform student engagement and literacy development through:

* **Reading Comprehension & Critical Analysis**: Students analyze texts from multiple perspectives, enhancing their analytical skills
* **Vocabulary & Language Skills**: Gaming contexts create authentic reasons to learn and use new terminology
* **Writing Skills & Creative Expression**: Character creation and narrative development foster writing fluency
* **Narrative Competence & Storytelling**: Students develop an intuitive understanding of story structures

My research connects theoretical foundations from play theory, developmental psychology, and literacy research with practical classroom applications. The goal? Creating a framework that any educator interested in game-based learning can adapt for their classroom.

## Character Analysis Role-Playing (CARP)

During my student teaching in 2023-24, I developed a lesson using concepts from tabletop RPGs to analyze characters in "The Most Dangerous Game." By adapting a solo TTRPG called "The Last Tea Shop Complete," I created an engaging approach to character analysis where students interviewed potential heroes or villains.

The results were honestly amazing—100% participation and creative audio recordings from students who normally wouldn't engage. This experience showed me how game-based approaches can transform traditionally passive learning activities into dynamic, engaging experiences.

Want to try it yourself? You can download the game instructions for free on [Teachers Pay Teachers](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Character-Analysis-Roleplaying-CARP-The-Most-Dangerous-Game-11492335).

## Educational Technology Perspective

My 25 years in technology before transitioning to education give me a unique perspective on educational technology. I'm both tech-savvy and critically aware of technology's limitations in the learning process. This background allows me to make thoughtful decisions about when and how to incorporate digital tools.

My approach to technology integration boils down to this simple philosophy:

> Technology should enhance human connection, not replace it. Digital tools should be used intentionally and strategically, serving specific learning goals rather than being used for their own sake.

Through my research and classroom innovations, I'm working to develop approaches that honor traditional literacy skills while preparing students for an increasingly digital future. The sweet spot isn't about choosing between old and new—it's about finding the perfect integration of both.
