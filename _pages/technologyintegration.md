## Technology Philosophy

As noted in my teaching philosophy, I believe in minimizing students' exposure to technology in the classroom, making it serve as a background tool rather than the primary means of delivery and engagement. This approach ensures that students engage with content more deeply and actively, fostering a more enriching learning environment.

Having spent 25 years in technology before transitioning to education, I bring a unique perspective to educational technology use. I am both technologically proficient and critically aware of technology's limitations and potential drawbacks in the learning process. This background allows me to make thoughtful decisions about when and how to incorporate digital tools.

My approach can be summarized as:

> Technology should enhance human connection, not replace it. Digital tools should be used intentionally and strategically, serving specific learning goals rather than being used for their own sake.

## Balancing Digital and Analog Learning

I deliberately create a balance between digital and analog learning experiences in my classroom:

**Digital** | **Analog**
--- | ---
Research tools | Physical books and printed materials
Digital recordings | Face-to-face discussions and presentations
Online submission and feedback | Handwritten drafts and peer editing
Strategic screen-based activities | Hands-on manipulatives and tactile learning

This balance is particularly important in today's educational landscape, where many students experienced digital overload during remote learning. My experience at Eastpointe Middle School highlighted this issue, as students expressed fatigue with "virtual work" (computer-based assignments). In response, I transformed assignments into physical formats, prioritizing student engagement despite the added grading complexity.

## Technology Integration Examples

My integration of technology is always purposeful and structured to support specific learning objectives:

### Character Analysis Role-Playing (CARP)

My CARP activity exemplifies my balanced approach to technology. The activity begins with analog components:
- Physical dice for random generation
- Handwritten journaling on paper sheets
- Face-to-face roleplaying and storytelling

Technology enters only at the final stage, when students use digital recording tools to create audio narratives based on their written work. This creates a purposeful technology integration that enhances rather than replaces human interaction, while also developing digital communication skills.

### Digital Literacy Instruction

While minimizing excessive screen time, I explicitly teach critical digital literacy skills:
- Evaluating online sources for credibility
- Understanding digital footprints and online identity
- Creating and sharing content responsibly
- Navigating digital texts and multimedia effectively

### Assessment and Feedback

I strategically use digital tools for assessment when they provide clear advantages:
- Audio recordings to assess speaking skills and preserve evidence of growth
- Google Forms for quick formative assessments that provide immediate data
- Shared documents for collaborative writing with visible revision histories
- Learning management systems for organizing resources and submissions

### Technology for Accessibility

Technology plays a crucial role in making learning accessible to all students:
- Text-to-speech and speech-to-text tools for students with learning differences
- Translation resources for English Language Learners
- Digital platforms that allow for flexible pacing
- Alternative formats for consuming and producing content

## Technology Decision Framework

When considering whether to integrate a technology tool, I ask these questions:

1. **What is the specific learning objective?**
2. **Does technology enhance or detract from achieving this objective?**
3. **Could a non-digital approach be more effective?**
4. **Does the technology promote active learning or passive consumption?**
5. **Is the technology accessible to all students?**
6. **Does the benefit outweigh the potential distraction?**

This framework helps ensure that technology serves learning, rather than learning being designed around technology.

## Remote Teaching Adaptations

During periods of remote or hybrid teaching, I adapted my approach to maintain engagement while mitigating screen fatigue:
- Breaking digital content into shorter segments
- Incorporating offline components into remote assignments
- Creating opportunities for synchronous interaction
- Designing asynchronous activities that allowed for flexibility
- Providing materials in multiple formats

## Future Exploration

While maintaining my balanced approach, I am exploring several promising areas of educational technology:

1. **Gaming and simulation tools** that connect to my thesis research on tabletop roleplaying games in education
2. **Audio and podcast creation** as alternatives to traditional written assignments
3. **Digital storytelling platforms** that allow students to craft multimedia narratives
4. **Virtual collaboration tools** that facilitate meaningful peer interaction
5. **Assistive technologies** that make learning more accessible for diverse learners

By continuing to evaluate and thoughtfully integrate technology while preserving the human elements of learning, I strive to prepare students for a digital world while developing their ability to engage deeply with content, think critically, and communicate effectively across multiple modalities.
