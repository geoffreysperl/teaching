## Overview

This page presents selected samples of student work from my classes. These artifacts demonstrate how my instructional approaches engage students and foster academic growth. Each sample includes context about the learning objectives, my instructional decisions, and reflections on outcomes.

**Learning Objectives:**
* Analyze how particular elements of a story interact (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.8.3)
* Analyze how dialogue propels the action and reveals aspects of character (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.8.3)
* Engage effectively in collaborative discussions (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.SL.8.1)

**Instructional Context:**  
This activity was implemented during a unit on Richard Connell's "The Most Dangerous Game." Traditional character analysis often relies on passive identification of traits rather than active engagement with the text. To address this, I developed CARP by adapting a solo tabletop roleplaying game (TTRPG) called "The Last Tea Shop Complete."

**Instructional Design:**  
Students interviewed one of two "potentials" (a hero or villain), who would become either Rainsford (protagonist) or Zaroff (antagonist). The activity progressed through:
1. Introduction to the TTRPG mechanics
2. Independent gameplay with guided Journal sheets
3. Audio recording of their character narratives
4. Reflection connecting their narratives to the original text

* **Student A's work** demonstrates sophisticated understanding of character motivation through their interpretation of potential villainous traits, particularly in how they connected decision-making to personality development.

**Learning Objectives:**
* Compare and contrast texts in different forms or genres (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.9-10.7)
* Analyze how authors' use of style and structure creates effects such as suspense or horror (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.9-10.5)
* Evaluate how modern authors draw on traditional works (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RL.9-10.9)

**Instructional Context:**  
During the 2023-24 school year, I taught a comparative analysis of Neil Gaiman's contemporary short story "Click-Clack the Rattlebag" and Edgar Allan Poe's classic "The Cask of Amontillado." This lesson allowed students to examine how horror techniques have evolved while recognizing the enduring narrative elements that create suspense and dread across different time periods.

**Instructional Design:**  
The lesson followed this structure:
1. Reading of both texts with guided questions
2. Small group discussions identifying key literary elements
3. Comparative analysis using the structured worksheet
4. Written response synthesizing students' analyses

**Student Work & Outcomes:**  
The sample worksheets show students' detailed analysis of both stories. Students identified and compared:
* Narrative techniques
* Development of suspense
* Use of setting and atmosphere
* Narrative perspective
* Character development and motivation

**Evidence of Learning:**  
* **Student 1's work** demonstrates sophisticated analysis of narrative perspective, noting how both authors use first-person narration to create unreliable narrators but with different effects.
* **Student 2's analysis** shows strong understanding of setting as a literary device, comparing Poe's catacombs to Gaiman's dark house and how both create claustrophobic atmospheres.
* **Student 3's responses** reveal deep engagement with character motivation, particularly in comparing the psychological aspects of both antagonists.

**Assessment & Outcomes:**  
Student feedback revealed high engagement with this comparative exercise. Their written analyses showed deeper understanding of literary techniques than previous assessments. However, upon reflection, I realized I had moved too quickly through the material for many students, despite their enthusiasm for the content.

**Teacher Reflection:**  
This lesson highlighted the importance of pacing in literary analysis. Though student engagement was high, I recognized that spreading the comparison over two class sessions would have allowed for deeper analysis. This experience informed my approach to pacing in subsequent lessons, ensuring I balance enthusiasm for the material with appropriate time for student processing and analysis.

**Learning Objectives:**
* Analyze the cultural and geographical connections across regions
* Interpret information presented visually (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.RH.6-8.7)
* Synthesize information from diverse sources (CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.W.6.8)

**Instructional Context:**  
At Eastpointe Middle School, I recognized that traditional assessments were not resonating with my 6th-grade geography students. Drawing inspiration from the culturally relevant Marvel "Black Panther" films, I designed this creative assessment to engage students with geographical concepts through a lens they found compelling.

**Instructional Design:**  
Students were tasked with:
1. Researching cultural and geographical features of African nations
2. Creating visual representations connecting their research to the fictional Wakanda
3. Writing explanatory text demonstrating their understanding
4. Participating in a gallery walk to view and respond to peers' work

**Student Work & Outcomes:**  
The sample artifacts show students' visual and textual representations of geographical concepts. Students integrated factual information about African geography with creative interpretations inspired by the fictional Wakanda.

**Evidence of Learning:**  
* Students demonstrated geographical knowledge through accurate representation of terrain, resources, and cultural elements
* Written components show understanding of how geography influences culture and development
* Visual representations reveal students' ability to synthesize information from multiple sources

**Assessment & Outcomes:**  
This creative assessment resulted in improved scores compared to traditional assessments on the same content. Students demonstrated stronger retention of geographical concepts and increased engagement with the material.

**Teacher Reflection:**  
This experience reinforced the value of connecting academic content to students' interests and cultural touchpoints. Using the Black Panther films as an entry point made geographical concepts more accessible and engaging for students. In future iterations, I would incorporate more structured peer feedback during the gallery walk phase to deepen analytical thinking.

## Conclusion

These samples represent my commitment to creating learning experiences that engage students through innovative approaches while maintaining rigorous academic standards. By connecting literature and geography to students' interests and providing multiple modes of expression, I strive to make learning accessible, meaningful, and engaging for all students.
