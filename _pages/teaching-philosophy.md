## Core Teaching Principles

My teaching philosophy centers on two fundamental beliefs that shape everything I do in the classroom:

### Individuality Matters

Every student is a unique constellation of talents, dreams, and challenges. I believe in celebrating their individuality through:

- Creating spaces where even the most reluctant learners can express themselves authentically
- Designing lessons with multiple entry points so everyone can find their way in
- Connecting content to what actually matters in students' lives
- Honoring the diverse backgrounds and experiences my students bring to our learning community

### Practical Skills with Heart

Drawing from my years in the business world, I'm big on helping students understand why literacy skills actually matter:

- Reading and listening aren't just about decoding words; they're about decoding life
- Writing and speaking aren't merely academic exercises; they're about bringing your thoughts into existence
- Even in this age of AI writing tools, these fundamental skills remain essential for success
- Language skills are tools for empowerment, enabling students to navigate and shape their worlds

## Teaching Approaches

These philosophical principles inform several key approaches in my day-to-day teaching:

### Reflective Practice

My background in IT taught me the value of continuous improvement. I've developed a structured reflection process that helps me make better teaching decisions. This reflection goes beyond just evaluating lessons—it's about understanding student needs and outcomes.

For example, when teaching Neil Gaiman's "Click-Clack the Rattlebag" alongside Poe's "The Cask of Amontillado," I noticed that despite the students' enthusiasm, I had moved too quickly through the material for many of them. This realization helped me develop a more thoughtful approach to pacing, especially for comparative literary analysis.

### Innovative Methods

I love building upon existing ideas and methods, adding new twists to established foundations. My Character Analysis Role-Playing (CARP) lesson is a perfect example. By adapting a solo tabletop roleplaying game, I created an engaging approach to character analysis where students interviewed potential literary characters. The result? 100% participation and some amazingly creative audio recordings.

### Equity & Inclusion

Teaching across metro Detroit has deepened my commitment to educational equity. At Eastpointe Middle School, I designed a culturally responsive assessment inspired by Marvel's *Black Panther* films. This Wakanda Gallery Walk connected geographical concepts to students' interests and improved assessment outcomes.

At Hamtramck High School, where I teach a wonderfully diverse population including many English Language Learners, I'm constantly adapting instruction to ensure everyone has equitable access to content through scaffolding, visual supports, and culturally diverse materials.

### Balanced Technology Integration

Despite my extensive tech background (or perhaps because of it), I believe in minimizing students' screen time in the classroom. Technology should serve as a background tool rather than the primary means of engagement. This approach ensures students engage with content more deeply and actively.

My CARP activity demonstrates this balanced approach. It begins with analog components (physical dice, handwritten journaling) and incorporates technology strategically (audio recording) only where it genuinely enhances the learning experience.
